Objective

BehaviorSelect one delivery platform for your mini-course and complete enough setup to host one lesson live behind a shareable link.
ConditionChoosing between exactly 2 real options (a hosted course platform versus your own site or existing tools), compared in writing against your actual constraints of budget, timeline, technical skill, and current audience size, all within a single working session.
CriterionOne full lesson (video plus its written 9-section content) is live and viewable by a test link by the end of the session, and you can state in one sentence why you chose this platform over the alternative.

Why This Matters

Platform choice is the single most common procrastination trap in course creation, because it looks like productive work while producing nothing shippable. Comparing six platforms for two weeks feels like progress and delivers zero lessons to zero learners. This is a business decision, not a technology decision: what ships a pilot to one committed learner fastest, given the constraints you actually have right now, not the platform you might need at scale someday.

The Technique

Compare exactly 2 real options, not six. A hosted course platform trades a percentage fee or subscription cost for speed: account, upload, publish, done same day, and it comes with delivery, payment, and access control built in. Building on your own site or existing tools trades that fee for full control, at the cost of your own time wiring together hosting, video delivery, and payment, none of which is your job to solve for a pilot of one.

Weigh the decision against your real constraints, not hypothetical ones: how much time do you actually have this week, what’s your current audience size (a custom site for one pilot buyer is a solved problem being over-solved), and how much of the fee matters at pilot volume versus at scale.

Default rule for a pilot: don’t build a website before you have paying or committed buyers. The hosted option almost always wins for a first pilot cohort, because the entire point of Lesson 11’s minimum viable launch is speed to a real learner, not infrastructure ownership.

Watch For This

Good

  • A written comparison of exactly 2 real options, each scored against your actual current constraints, not generic pros and cons lifted from a blog post.
  • A platform is chosen and one lesson is live behind a test link inside the same session.
  • The one-sentence reason for the choice names a real constraint (time, current audience size, cost at pilot volume), not “it looked nicer.”

Classic Failure

  • The session gets spent comparing 5 or 6 platforms in depth and nothing ships.
  • A custom site gets built for a pilot audience of one buyer, solving a scale problem that doesn’t exist yet.
  • The platform is chosen for its feature list instead of for what gets a lesson live today.

Your Drill

Compare exactly 2 real platform options in writing (2 to 3 sentences each covering cost, control, and time to launch) against your actual current constraints. Pick one. Set it up and get one full lesson (video plus written content) live behind a shareable test link before the session ends.

Pass Checklist

Lesson complete

Coach Note

If you’re still comparing platforms after this session, that’s not diligence, that’s the polite version of not wanting to ship yet. Pick the one that gets you live today and change platforms later if the pilot proves the course deserves the investment.
